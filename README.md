Project Overview:
A company that provides credit card services earns revenue from transaction fees and interest charges. Customers who maintain a zero balance do not contribute to this revenue stream because they are not actively using their cards. To address this, we want to target inactive customers with promotional offers, with the intention of encouraging them to make purchases with their credit cards. Once spending is initiated, it can generate revenue for the provider. 
The dataset provided contains customer information, including demographic attributes such as age and income which are collected during the application process, and also includes records indicating whether customers responded to previous promotional campaigns conducted by the company. 
K-Means clustering was used to segment customers into subgroups to identify the subgroup that contains a higher proportion of customers with no card balance to send promotional materials to, as sending promotional materials to all customers is not cost-effective.
Apriori association rule mining was used to reveal customer preferences for promotional offers within the target group.

Model Evaluation:
For K-Means clustering, we compared the average silhouette coefficients of several K-values, and used a K-value that yields the highest average silhouette coefficient. We also applied the principle of parsimony, choosing the smallest K-value that generated useful clusters in this specific business scenario. Outliers were also present in the data, but were deemed as valid during data exploration. Since K-Means algorithm is sensitive to outliers, we compared the results of the average silhouette coefficients between models that included, and excluded outliers but the difference was negligible so we decided to use the model where outliers were included as they are valid rather than erroneous. 
For Apriori association rule mining, we decided on an optimal minimum support value to include promotional events that customers are likely to engage in and excluded promotional events where customers are less likely to engage in as they can lead to potentially low turnout rates affecting the return on investment of our marketing campaign. Additionally, we selected the optimal minimum confidence threshold that produced actionable, meaningful business insights.
